San Francisco

Lurie and City Administrator Chu use their new Prop. E powers for the first time: one citywide rulebook for $6 billion a year in contracting

The standards ban sharing confidential info with favored vendors, require conflict-of-interest disclosures, route contractor contact through official channels, and block splitting purchases to dodge competitive bidding. Rules take effect automatically after 60 days unless the mayor or a majority of supervisors blocks them.

“Every day, dedicated public servants make decisions about how San Francisco spends taxpayer dollars. They deserve clear rules, practical guidance, and the confidence to speak up when something doesn't seem right.” - Carmen Chu, SF City Administrator

San Francisco

Supervisors vote unanimously to legalize home restaurants: MEHKO permits let residents sell home-cooked food from their kitchens, carts or stalls

Permits cost $828 a year with a health inspection; home operations are capped at 90 meals a week and $100,000 in annual sales (200 and $200,000 for pushcarts). The vote follows a commissary-kitchen requirement vendors said priced them out at ~$1,000 a month.

“This legislation will help break down barriers for aspiring food entrepreneurs, expand economic opportunities for immigrant-owned businesses and street vendors, and preserve the diverse culinary traditions that make San Francisco so special.” - Jackie Fielder, District 9 Supervisor
