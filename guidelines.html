Editorial Guidelines

← Jacob's Daily Digest

This digest is built for one reader. These are the principles it follows, and what you can hold it to.

Headlines

  • Headlines carry the specifics: names, numbers, dates, scores, versions, and active verbs.
  • Scanning the headlines alone should catch you up on the day. Clicking is for wanting more, never for finding out what a story is even about.
  • No teasers. Nothing important is ever withheld to earn a click.
  • No inflation. A story is as big as it is, and the headline says so.
  • The most newsworthy fact leads. If a source buries it, the digest un-buries it.

Accuracy

  • Every link goes to a real page. Nothing is ever invented: no links, no quotes, no claims, no numbers.
  • Quotes are verbatim, with the speaker's name and role attached.
  • Every story links its sources so you can check anything in one tap.

Photos

  • Every story has a photo when the source has a real one.
  • When no real photo exists, an illustration is generated and labeled as such. It never pretends to be photography of the event.

Selection

  • Ten to fifteen items a day, curated sharp over volume. No duplicates, no filler.
  • The beats: Chiefs, Nebraska football, AI and agent tooling, running, and San Francisco.
  • Roundups lead with the most newsworthy thing that shipped, not with the word "roundup".

Feedback

  • Every reaction is one tap: Loved it, Meh, Less of this, and Chat about it sit under every story, and a quick survey rides along each day. Taps text straight to Instinct - no typing required.
  • Wrong about something? Say so. Corrections ship with the next build.
